# Changelog

All notable changes to BSP-InventorySync are recorded here. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/); versions follow [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/).

## [Unreleased]

### Added
- **An api for staff tools** (Server Utilities' staff web panel, its request SU-IS2), `InventorySyncApi.API_VERSION` 2, documented in `docs/API.md` under "Staff tools":
  - `read(UUID)`: a player's items as areas of slots: inventory, armour, offhand, cursor, ender chest, Curios slots, the item slots of every carried Sophisticated Backpack (with where each sits: "in ender chest", "inside Shulker Box", inside another backpack) and Dank Storage danks. Live for a player on this server, otherwise their newest snapshot, with its version, whether they are online and where. A Shatter Totem is shown locked, as an inert copy; a missing mod's item is named; a linked-storage backpack is read-only.
  - `edit(UUID, expectedVersion, Edit, by)`: add a number of an item (all or nothing), or remove a number from one slot that must still hold the item seen. Live on this server, into the inventory, ender chest, a backpack or a dank through the mods' own rules, saved at once with the state before pinned; offline on the newest snapshot as `/invsync edit` offline does, into the inventory or ender chest (backpacks and danks only give items up there); refused for a player online on another server. Answers `OK`, `ONLINE`, `CHANGED`, `BUSY`, `NO_ROOM`, `REFUSED` or `FAILED`, with a line for staff and the saved and pinned versions. Never adds or removes a live Shatter Totem, not even inside another item, and never adds an item carrying a backpack's or dank's contents id.
  - `saveNow(ServerPlayer)`: saves a player now, so a change made to them some other way survives a crash before the next auto-save.
  - Each change is logged as "panel: <by>" and noted inside its admin snapshot.

### Fixed
- Taking a player's checkout is now one step in the store, taken only when nobody else holds it (`SyncStore.claim`). Before, a player logging in at the very moment an offline `/invsync edit` was written from another server could get the snapshot from before the edit, and the edit was then lost to their next save. The arrival now waits for that server to finish; an offline edit claims the checkout before it checks the version; and an arrival on the server writing the edit waits too. The release lands before the edit answers, and a player who leaves while their arrival's claim is still on its way is released only after it (with Redis, the other order left them held for every other server).
- `/invsync edit` offline saved its change under the editing server's id, so a player's dank from the server they last played on could take the contents of another dank on the editing server at their next login there. A change is now saved under the server id of the snapshot it changes. It also waits for another change of the same player on the same server and for that server's own save of them, reports a failed pin as such, and no longer carries a staff panel's note onto its own snapshot.

### Upgrading
- Update every backend together: an older build still takes the checkout the old way, so the fix holds only once every server has it. The stored format is unchanged (a staff change adds an optional `Note` to its snapshot, which older builds ignore).
- Every client needs this jar (the exact version rule).

## [0.6.0] - 2026-11-08

### Changed
- **The headstone is bigger**: a block wide and a little over a block tall (base 16 by 2 by 8, stone 14 by 14 and 4 deep, cap to 19/16), standing across the back half of its block. What you bump into and click follows the stone, its base and the head in front. The floating text sits higher to match. A grave now needs headroom for its cap: a spot whose block above has something to bump into or reaches where the cap goes (a space one block high, a slab, scaffolding, powder snow) is skipped for the nearest spot with room, so a death in a low tunnel can leave its grave just outside it or on the surface above (the death message gives the place, as before).
- A glowing golden sigil on the stone's front and back, drawn full-bright (it shows at night and underground); the block's own light stays 4. Model and the sigil texture (`grave_sigil.png`) come from `tools/gen_grave_assets.py`.

### Added
- **The owner's head** out of the ground in front of the stone, up to the chin, with their own skin as it was at the death (Steve or Alex until it loads), leaning 18 degrees to the left or right, chosen from the grave's id so a row of graves differs. It is solid and part of the grave: a right-click on it opens the grave. A grave keeps only the skin from the owner's profile. Graves from 0.5.0 lean left and show the default skin for their owner.
- Gold and neon green motes drifting up around a grave, about 3 a second, for each player within 16 blocks, made on that player's client so their Particles setting applies; the server sends nothing.
- `tools/gen_grave_preview.py` (a render of the block from its own models) and the mock-ups of D26 and D27 (`tools/gen_grave_detail_concepts.py`).

### Upgrading
- Every client needs this jar (the exact version rule): ship it in the pack at the same time as on the servers.
- Graves put up by 0.5.0 take the new look where they stand. One already standing in a space one block high shows its cap in the ceiling block (the headroom rule only chooses where new graves go); it opens and empties as before.

## [0.5.0] - 2026-11-07

### Added
- **Graves**, taking over from Simply Graves (remove Simply Graves from the servers and the pack; while it is installed these graves stay off, so nothing is handled twice). A death that is not PvP leaves a headstone where it happened, holding everything that would have dropped and the experience points that would have dropped as orbs. Every death gets its own grave, so a second death never touches the first.
- The headstone faces the way the player faced, is made of the stone that suits the place (blackstone in the Nether, end stone in the End, deepslate below y 0, stone elsewhere; vanilla textures), glows faintly (light 4) and shows the owner's name and "Right-click to open", or "Public in 58:12", over it on each player's screen. Nothing removes it: breaking is refused in every game mode (on the client too), explosions of any power, pistons, water, lava, fire, the wither, the dragon and growing trees leave it, FTB Chunks claims and protected regions never keep a player out, Botania's Gaia fight spares it, and Carry On and Mekanism's cardboard box are told not to lift it. It has no item and drops nothing. Models, block state, lang line and tags come from `tools/gen_grave_assets.py`.
- Where it stands: the death spot, brought down to the ground; in water, lava or a wall, the nearest dry spot within `graves.searchRadius` (8) blocks; never where BSP-Core's dropped Shatter Totem lies, and never where the End rebuilds itself (the arrival platform, the exit portal's podium, above the obsidian pillars). Failing that, where the player last stood on solid ground; failing that, kept without a block and recovered with `/grave`.
- Right-click opens a six-row chest screen laid out like the inventory it came from. Click a stack to take it, or Take all: each stack goes back to the slot it came from when that is free (armour is worn again), the rest into the main inventory, part of a stack when only part fits, never deleted in creative mode; what does not fit stays in the grave. Nothing can be put in. Only the owner may open it for `graves.privateMinutes` (60), then anyone; the first to open it gets the experience; the owner is told on whichever server they are when someone else opens it. The last stack taken removes the block.
- `/grave` lists a player's graves on every server, newest first; [Recover] (`/grave recover <id>`) opens one that found no spot, whose block a crash lost, or that nobody opened for `graves.recoverAfterDays` (7), wherever the player is. Backpack and dank contents travel with their items.
- Admins (level 2, and the console): `/invsync graves <player>` lists every grave with its id; `/invsync grave open <player> <id>` opens it; `/invsync grave give <player> <id> <to>` makes it `to`'s (no block; they recover it with `/grave`, contents and experience included); `/invsync grave remove <player> <id>` empties it for good, with a log line listing every stack.
- A grave nobody opened for `graves.archiveAfterDays` (30) is put away (its block goes, its owner no longer sees it, admins still can); `graves.deleteArchivedAfterDays` (30) later it is deleted, with every stack in the log.
- No grave for a PvP death (killed by another player, directly or with a projectile, as BSP-Core counts it): the items and orbs drop for the killer. No grave either while the player's data is still arriving, and no live Shatter Totem in any grave.
- Storage: the new table `bsp_is_graves` (created at start) on synced servers, this world's own data on a server without the network database (single player included). On a synced server a new grave is written in the same transaction as the player's next snapshot, saved at once after the death: the grave and the empty-handed snapshot land together or not at all, so a crash, a failed write or a lost answer can never leave the items both in the grave and back in the inventory, or in neither. Every change to a grave is made at the revision read and checked again when the database answers with an error, so two players or two servers never take the same stack and a late answer is never mistaken for a refusal. An emptied grave stays as a marker for 30 days, so no old copy can bring it back. Stacks a player took but could not receive (they left, died or filled their inventory while the database answered) wait in a new grave of theirs.
- `/invsync status` shows whether graves are on and where they are kept; `/invsync reload` reads the `graves` settings again.
- Config section `graves`: `enabled` (true), `searchRadius` (8), `privateMinutes` (60), `recoverAfterDays` (7), `archiveAfterDays` (30), `deleteArchivedAfterDays` (30); item tag `bsp_inventorysync:no_grave` (empty) for items that should drop as usual.

### Changed
- **The mod is now needed on every client, in exactly the server's version** (the grave block lives on both sides). A server refuses a player without it or with another version, with Forge's mismatch screen naming BSP InventorySync. A client with the mod can still join servers that do not have it. Every update of this mod now needs a pack update at the same time.
- The chest screens' picture slots (the grave screen and the waiting-items popup of `/invsync give`) refuse taking and placing outright, so a mod that moves items without a click (a sort or deposit button) cannot take a copy out.

## [0.4.0] - 2026-11-07

### Added
- `/invsync give <player> <item> [count]` (operator level 2, and the console): gives an item on any server of the network. The item as vanilla `/give` takes it (tab completion, NBT); at most 100 stacks' worth; Shatter Totems and items no snapshot carries are refused. A player on that server whose data has arrived gets what fits at once. Everything else waits in the new table `bsp_is_gives` (created at start), one row per stack: the server the player is on hands it over within `give.pollSeconds` (5), and an offline player gets it after their data arrives at their next login, on any server. A row is removed from the database just before its stack goes into the inventory, so it can never arrive twice. Gifts go into the hotbar and main inventory directly, never through vanilla's insert, which deletes what does not fit in creative mode. The giver is told where it went ("on the way to BSP-Frostwarf", "waiting for their next login"), the player what they received and from whom, and the log gets one line.
- The waiting-items popup: when gifts do not fit, the player sees a three-row chest screen (nothing to install) with up to 18 waiting stacks, Cancel, an info paper saying how many free slots are still needed, and Deposit. Their own inventory below works, so they can make room right there; Deposit puts in what fits and keeps the rest showing; when everything is in, it closes. Cancel or Esc puts the rest off until their next join to the network (remembered on every server); a server switch through BSP-Teleport is not a join, and a logout or switch with the popup open is not a Cancel. It opens only when no other screen is open.
- Hand-overs are safe against a failing database and a player who leaves: a stack whose removal from the database fails stays waiting and the rest are still handed over (the player is never left without deliveries, and Deposit never stops working); a player who logs out, switches or dies while the database answers gets nothing put into the copy already saved, and the stack waits again (if even that fails, the log names the item so it can be given again). A server reads a player's Cancel before handing them anything, so `/invsync reload` or a failed read never asks again someone who cancelled. A give that the database only partly stores says how many went in, how many wait and how many to give again. Closing the popup while a Deposit is answered decides the Cancel on what is really left.
- Config `give.pollSeconds` (5): how often each server looks for items waiting for its players.
- `/invsync edit <player> [word]` (operator level 3): an inventory editor for a player online on the same server, in a vanilla chest screen (nothing to install). Tabs for the inventory (main, hotbar, armour, offhand), the ender chest and a creative item list (by creative tab, 45 a page, or filtered by the word); a toolbar with Trash, Cancel and Save; Save shows what is added and removed, and Confirm makes the change. The admin's own inventory is locked and the cursor item is dropped when the screen closes, so nothing is carried out. Confirm is refused if the player's inventory changed meanwhile; with the sync on, the state before is saved and pinned and the edit saved as an admin snapshot (`/invsync restore` undoes it), and one log line names the admin, the player and the changes. A player's Shatter Totem can be trashed; the list never offers totems. A player who is offline is edited on their newest snapshot: Confirm saves the edit as their newest (admin) snapshot and pins the one it started from, holding their checkout in the store while it writes so a server they join meanwhile waits for it; it is refused if they came online anywhere or a newer snapshot was saved meanwhile, and every other part of the snapshot (and items of mods this server lacks) is kept as it was. A player online on another server is refused ("edit them there").
- Project skeleton: Forge 47.4.10 / Minecraft 1.20.1 Gradle build, mod id `bsp_inventorysync`, BSP-Core as a required dependency, a `@Mod` entry class that only logs.
- Storage layer: the `SyncStore` interface (snapshots per player with versions, causes, pinning and rotation; who is online where; extra blobs for data kept outside the player's NBT, such as backpack contents), `Snapshot`, `Presence`, `SaveCause`, and the in-memory `MemoryStore` used by the tests and by servers without a shared database.
- Forge GameTests (`./gradlew runGameTestServer`) for the storage rules, with the empty template written by `tools/gen_gametest_structures.py`.
- Data modules: `DataModule` and the `Modules` registry, `PlayerSnapshotCodec` (one compound per module, GZIP-compressed NBT per snapshot, every module isolated so one failure never costs the rest), `ItemRules` (items a snapshot never carries) and `ItemCodec` (an item whose mod is missing becomes a named sheet of paper holding the original NBT, and turns back into the item when saved again).
- Vanilla modules: inventory (main, armour, offhand, selected slot, cursor), ender chest, experience, game (game mode, abilities, respawn point), vitals (health, absorption, air, hunger, effects), advancements (through the player's advancements file, no rewards re-granted).
- GameTests for every module's round trip, the placeholder rule, item exclusions, module isolation and the GZIP bytes, through a mock server player.
- Totem rules: a live Shatter Totem is never written into a snapshot (`compat/BspCore` installs the rule, reaching BSP-Core's api when present and its internal check otherwise); a restore keeps every live totem the player holds and gives it back through BSP-Core afterwards, never dropping one.
- `SyncService`: save a player to the store and restore the newest snapshot, posting the api events.
- Public api (`docs/API.md`): `InventorySyncApi` (`registerModule`, `excludeItems`, `isSyncing`, `API_VERSION` 1), `DataModule`, `InventoryRestoredEvent`, `InventorySavedEvent`.
- GameTests for the totem rules and the events (17 in all).
- The arrival handshake: an arriving player is frozen (no movement, interaction, pickup, drop, block breaking or damage) while the server waits for the one they came from to finish saving, then gets the newest snapshot; a configurable timeout applies the last known snapshot with a warning or refuses the login. Players who leave mid-sync are never saved; dead arrivals are restored at the respawn; deaths save at the respawn; a world save saves every settled player. Server start and stop release the players this server held.
- `beginTransfer` / `cancelTransfer` in the api for BSP-Teleport (an early snapshot before the proxy switch).
- Server config `bsp_inventorysync-server.toml`: `storage.mode` (`off` by default), `storage.serverId`, `arrival.timeoutSeconds`, `arrival.kickOnTimeout`.
- GameTests for the handshake and its races, with two services sharing one store (24 in all).
- The MySQL store (`JdbcStore`): plain JDBC on one worker thread, no bundled driver (BSP-Core's full jar or Mohist provides one), tables `players`, `snapshots` and `extra` under the `bsp_is_` prefix, created on start. Database settings taken from BSP-Core's own `storage.*` config by default.
- The Redis link (`RedisClient`, this mod's own small client, nothing bundled) and `FastStore`: the checkout and the newest snapshot of every player live in Redis too, so a server switch never waits on MySQL; MySQL stays the source of truth and the safety net when Redis is down.
- Config: `storage.useBspCoreDatabase`, `storage.host/port/database/user/password/useSsl`, `storage.tablePrefix`, `storage.snapshotsKept` (16); `redis.enabled/host/port/username/password/cluster/snapshotTtlSeconds`. The server id falls back to BSP-Core's `storage.serverId`.
- Snapshots are pruned after every save to the configured count; pinned ones are kept.
- GameTests for the Redis client (against a fake Redis on a local socket, with an outage), the fast store's cache and fallback, the handshake through the fast store, and the JDBC store against nothing listening (28 in all).
- The generic Forge data module: every mod's per-player persistent NBT (`ForgeData`) and capabilities (`ForgeCaps`) travel with the player, with exclusion lists in the config (`sync.excludeForgeData`, `sync.excludeCapabilities`); excluded paths keep this server's values.
- Adapters, each only when its mod is loaded and none compiled against: Curios (worn curios through Curios' own tag), Sophisticated Backpacks (contents by `contentsUuid`, including backpacks inside backpacks, written into this server's storage), Dank Storage (contents by frequency; a dank from another server gets a fresh frequency here).
- GameTests for the generic module (persistent NBT with exclusions, capabilities through Forge with a test capability), the backpack UUID scan and the restore context (32 in all).
- Auto-save: every `storage.autosaveSeconds` (60) each settled player is captured and written only if anything changed since their last save; unchanged players cost one capture and no database call. The advancements file is reused until a criterion changes.
- The watchdog: any database or Redis call that takes longer than `storage.timeoutSeconds` (5) is given up as failed, so a frozen database never leaves a player frozen, a save hanging or the shutdown waiting.
- GameTests for the auto-save, the advancements dirty flag and the watchdog against a store that never answers (35 in all).
- `/invsync` for operators: `status`, `reload` (re-reads the config, reopens the stores, adopts the players online), `save <player>`, `resync <player>`, `dump <player>` (per-module sizes and the full SNBT written under `world/bsp_inventorysync/dumps/`), `snapshots <player>`, `restore <player> <version>`, `pin` and `unpin <player> <version>`.
- GameTests for the actions and the command wiring (40 in all).
- The start-up log line and `/invsync status` say where the database settings came from (BSP-Core's config or this mod's).

### Changed
- Compiled against BSP-Core 1.6.2 (`bsp_core_version`); any BSP-Core from 1.1.0 on is still accepted.
- With BSP-Core 1.4.1 or later the totem rule and the totem hand-back use BSP-Core's api (`BspTotems.isTotem(ItemStack)`, `BspTotems.giveBack(ServerPlayer, ItemStack)`); the start-up log says "totem rule via api, hand-back via api". Older BSP-Core versions keep using its internal methods as before. A restore into a full inventory keeps every totem the player holds; BSP-Core drops the stack each one displaces at the player's feet (GameTest `a_restore_into_a_full_inventory_keeps_every_totem_and_drops_what_they_displace`).
- `InventoryRestoredEvent` now also fires once, with version 0, when a player logs in on a server where the sync is off (or its store failed to start). BSP-Core 1.4.1 holds coins from an older season, the season check and owed items until it sees this event (request R3), so without it every login on such a server waited `storage.restoreWaitSeconds` (30 s). GameTest `bsp_core_settles_a_login_on_the_one_restore_event_with_the_sync_on_or_off` checks BSP-Core's wait ends on the event with the sync off and on.
- A player who arrives dead gets their restore event after the respawn, on the living player; with BSP-Core 1.4.1 (request R4) BSP-Core keeps waiting through the death screen and hands owed items and totems to the respawned player. GameTest `a_dead_arrival_past_bsp_cores_wait_gets_its_data_and_owed_totem_after_the_respawn`.
- With BSP-Core 1.4.2 (request R5) a Shatter Totem that BSP-Core holds for a player while their restore is pending reaches them through BSP-Core's totem route after the restore: never dropped, even into a full inventory, and recorded so BSP-Core's identity check leaves it live. Use BSP-Core 1.4.2 or later with this mod: on 1.4.1 such a totem is dropped in a full inventory, and otherwise spent by the identity check. GameTest `a_full_inventory_switch_gets_the_owed_totem_by_bsp_cores_totem_route_and_it_stays_live`.
- Snapshots are compressed and decompressed on a background thread (`bsp-inventorysync-codec`), in order per player; only the capture and the apply stay on the server thread.
- The world save writes only players whose data changed since their last save, so an idle player's rollback history is no longer filled with identical snapshots.
- At most four players are captured for the auto-save per tick, and players adopted at a reload (or seen first while online) get their first auto-save at a random point of the interval, so a crowd is never captured in one tick.
- While the database cannot be reached, a new connection is tried at most once per back-off (1 s doubling to 15 s) and calls in between fail at once, so the queue no longer grows during an outage. Table set-up warns when the database's `max_allowed_packet` is under 16 MB, and a snapshot over 4 MB compressed is logged.
- A player waiting more than a second for their data sees "Loading your items..." on the action bar. A failed presence read is logged once per arrival, not every tick. The arrival-timeout message adds "Tell an admin if anything is missing."; a player whose modules failed to load is told which. The placeholder's lore now reads "Turns back into the item on a server with its mod. Do not craft with it."
- The respawn point (bed or anchor) is no longer synced (decision D16): each server keeps the one set in its own world, as vanilla does. A point from another world pointed nowhere, was cleared at the next death, and the cleared point was carried back. Game mode and abilities still travel; a respawn point in an older snapshot is ignored.
- Placeholders for items whose mod a server lacks are now a named barrier instead of paper (decision D14): no recipe, villager trade, furnace or composter uses it, so the original can no longer be crafted or traded away. Right-clicking with one is refused ("This is a placeholder for a missing mod's item." on the action bar); clicking a block with one in hand still works on the block. The lore reads "Turns back into the item on a server with its mod." and "Keep it: it is the real item." Paper placeholders made before this are still recognised and become barriers at their next restore. CurseForge texts and the `missing-items` and `what-travels` images updated (the latter also for D16).

### Fixed
- **The inventory editor ignored every click from an admin in spectator mode.** Minecraft throws away a spectator's clicks in any inventory screen and only sends the screen again, so `/invsync edit` opened but its tabs never switched and no item could be moved. The editor now refuses to open for an admin in spectator mode ("Leave spectator mode to use the editor ..."), for online and offline players alike, and closes without changing anything if its admin switches to spectator while it is open. An offline player's editor checks again once their snapshot has been read, so switching to spectator during the read still refuses it; the close follows the game mode other mods settle on (it runs after them, not for a switch they cancel, and also when they turn another switch into spectator).
- **The XP bar showed 0, and status effects vanished, after a teleport into another dimension** (a vanilla 1.20.1 bug, fixed here because every server runs this mod). A teleport through `ServerPlayer.teleportTo` into another dimension (vanilla `/tp`, BSP-Teleport, BSP-Core's totem recall, Region Guard's border links and triggers) rebuilds the player on their client with no experience and no effects and never sends either again: the bar stayed empty until the player's XP changed (it came back on the next portal trip), and the effects were gone from the client (icons, night vision, and the movement the client predicts itself: slow falling, levitation, jump boost) while the server still had them. The player's experience and active effects are now sent again on every dimension change, whether the sync is on or off. Their real experience and effects on the server were never lost and are untouched.
- **A server id in `sync.networkServers` spelled in another case was not matched.** On the live network the list said "BSP-Saferoom" while SafeRoom's id is "BSP-SafeRoom", so a player arriving from SafeRoom got an older snapshot from another server (items and BSP-Core's saved data rolled back). Server ids are now matched ignoring case, as Velocity matches its server names; a snapshot whose server matched only that way is applied and logged once per id ("correct the spelling in the list"), and a list entry that is this server's own id in another case is logged at start.
- **BSP-Core's server-local keys could travel.** `PlayerPersisted.bsp_core:LastMainServer` (the server the saved main-dimension position belongs to) and `PlayerPersisted.bsp_core:ZoneHeldTotems` (live Shatter Totems BSP-Core keeps through a death in an Anti Totem zone or on a no-totem server) were saved into snapshots and applied on other servers. Both, with `LastMainDim` and `LastMainPos`, are now always kept on the server that wrote them, even if removed from `sync.excludeForgeData`; the list's default shows all four, and anything else it lists is excluded as well. Existing config files keep their old list, which is now harmless.
- **A restore that failed outside the data modules let the player play on a half-applied inventory.** No restore event was posted (BSP-Core waited its full `storage.restoreWaitSeconds`), and the half-applied state could be saved over the player's real data later. The login is now refused as when their data cannot be read ("Your items could not be loaded from the network ... please rejoin in a minute"), nothing of theirs is saved on that server, and any Shatter Totem taken for the restore is given back first. A listener of `InventoryRestoredEvent` that throws is logged and no longer stops the login or the other listeners.
- **The database and Redis passwords reached every player.** Forge sends each SERVER config file to every player at login, and this mod's connection settings (database host, port, name, user, password, SSL; Redis host, port, user, password) were in `bsp_inventorysync-server.toml`. They now live in a common config, `config/bsp_inventorysync-network.toml`, which is never sent. At the first start the values are moved there once (a value already set in the new file wins), the old keys are cleared and left as "Moved to ..." placeholders, and the log names the keys that moved, never a value. Change the database and Redis passwords after updating. `storage.useBspCoreDatabase` is unchanged and now names BSP-Core's `config/bsp_core-network.toml` as its source.
- **A single-player world could sync with the network.** A client modpack that ships the servers' `bsp_inventorysync-server.toml` in `defaultconfigs` gives every new single-player world `storage.mode = "mysql"` and a real server's id; on a machine that can reach the database, that world saved its inventory as the player's newest snapshot, which the network then applied (and released that server's players at its start and stop). The sync now runs only on a dedicated server: single-player and LAN worlds are always off, with the log line "not a dedicated server (single player or LAN): inventory sync is off".
- **Snapshots from servers outside the network were applied.** New setting `sync.networkServers`: the server ids of the network's backends. A snapshot saved by any other server is passed over on arrival and by `/invsync resync` (logged), and the newest snapshot from a listed server is applied instead. Empty (the default) keeps the old behaviour and logs a warning at start.
- **Server stop saved nobody.** Forge fires `ServerStoppingEvent` before Minecraft logs the players out, and the sync stopped there, so at every restart the players online were not saved and came back on their last auto-save (up to a minute old: gains lost, used or dropped items back). The sync now stops at `ServerStoppedEvent`, after every logout has saved.
- **The item on the cursor was duplicated at logout and server switch.** It went into the snapshot and Minecraft also dropped it where the player stood. Cursor and 2x2 crafting-grid items now go into the inventory before the logout snapshot; what does not fit is left out of the snapshot and dropped as before, so it is in one place only. A server switch through BSP-Teleport closes the open menu first, as any close does.
- **Stale items could be duplicated while frozen.** Items moved from the arriving server's old copy into the 2x2 crafting grid survived the restore; the grid is now emptied when the snapshot is applied. Commands are refused while a player's data is still arriving (a command acting on the old copy, such as a bank deposit, could be undone by the restore), with "Your items are still loading. Try again in a moment."
- **An arrival whose data could not be read played on, and later saved, the old copy.** The login is now refused ("Your items could not be loaded from the network. Nothing was changed: please rejoin in a minute.") and nothing of that server's copy is ever saved. The same applies to a player refused at the arrival timeout (`arrival.kickOnTimeout = true`).
- **A late release could undo another server's checkout.** Marking a player offline now happens only where the store still names this server (MySQL, the in-memory store and Redis, including the release of everyone at server start).
- **A quick relog to the same server could read the snapshot before its own logout save landed**; the arrival now waits for this server's saves of the player. The release after a logout now waits for the store's real answer, not the watchdog's timeout.
- **A stale Redis copy could be served as the newest snapshot** (a save while Redis was down left the older cached copy for up to `redis.snapshotTtlSeconds`), handing back items already dropped or stored. The cached copy is now checked against the database's newest version.
- **Data of a mod missing on one server was lost on the way through it.** Modules this server does not have, modules that failed this time, and capabilities of mods missing here are now carried forward from the last snapshot.
- **A Shatter Totem in the ender chest was wiped by a restore**; it is now kept and given back through BSP-Core like the others.
- **Backpacks worn in a Curios slot or inside a shulker box (or another container item) were not synced**; the backpack scan now looks through whole item tags and the Curios slots. A Dank on the cursor gets its contents back too.
- **Health above the bare maximum was cut on arrival** (gear bonuses apply a tick later); it is given back two ticks after the restore.
- **An admin rollback (`/invsync restore`) was not saved**: it is now saved at once as the newest snapshot, and the reply says so. `save`, `resync`, `restore` and `dump` refuse a player whose data is still arriving; `/invsync reload` refuses while anyone is still arriving.
